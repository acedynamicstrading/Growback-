Oyster mushroom's spawn-run time, temperature/humidity targets, and yield are taken directly from the Department of Agriculture Sri Lanka's published cultivation table. King oyster isn't one of DOA's released varieties, so those figures come from general Pleurotus eryngii literature instead and carry more uncertainty — flagged as such below. Seasonal misting guidance follows DOA's Wet Zone / Dry Zone rainfall classification, since the west and east of the island are wet and dry at opposite times of year. Your own strain, substrate batch, and microclimate will still shift the exact numbers — treat this as a solid, sourced starting plan, not a guarantee.

When things go wrong

Common complications, and how to fix them

No pins after colonization finishes

Usually low fresh-air exchange or humidity too low to trigger fruiting.

Open vents or slit the bag, mist lightly twice a day, and give it a few degrees of temperature drop if you can — that swing is often the actual trigger.

Green or black patches on the substrate

Mould contamination — DOA identifies this as Trichoderma (green) or Aspergillus niger (black), usually from unclean handling or under-sterilised substrate.

Isolate that unit away from the others immediately and discard it — it's not salvageable. DOA's prevention checklist for next time: standard sawdust/rice bran/cotton waste substrate, proper bag sterilisation, disease-free spawn, sterile inoculation conditions, and steady temperature/humidity through the run.

Wet, slimy substrate with a sour smell

Bacterial infection, usually from substrate that was too saturated going in.

The Department of Agriculture's own remedy: rinse with chlorinated water (about 10g of freely-available chlorine per litre of water) every 3–5 days. Cut away small isolated patches; discard the unit if it's spread through, and drain substrate longer before bagging next time.

Long thin stems, tiny caps ("leggy" growth)

Too little fresh air exchange — CO₂ has built up around the fruiting bodies.

Increase ventilation — open flaps or vents longer and more often, or add a small fan for gentle air movement nearby.

Pins form, then shrivel and stop

Humidity dropped after pinning, or a direct draft dried them out before they could establish.

Mist more consistently through this stage and keep any fan or vent pointed away from directly hitting the young pins.

Mushrooms turning yellow

Usually overwatering combined with stale air, sometimes just natural ageing past peak harvest.

Cut back misting, improve airflow, and harvest promptly once caps flatten rather than waiting.

Small flies or mites around the setup

Mushroom flies (Bradysia, Cecid, Phorid, Sciarid) and mites are drawn to overripe substrate or spilled organic matter — the Department of Agriculture names these as the main oyster mushroom pests in Sri Lanka.

DOA's own controls: build or line your cropping area with insect-proof net, keep it scrupulously clean, and spray neem or garlic extract. For mites specifically, a sulphur-based insecticide is their recommendation. Remove spent substrate promptly either way.

Colonization is taking much longer than expected

Usually too cold, or substrate moisture was off going in.

Move the unit somewhere steadier around 24–27°C. If it's been more than twice the expected window with no visible mycelium spread, check for contamination instead.
